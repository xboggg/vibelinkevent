Her Life

A woman who planted

Akua Sika Adomako began her career at Achimota Botanic Gardens in 1991 as a junior researcher. She left in 2008 to become biology teacher at her old school, Wesley Girls', because she believed teaching was the longer way to change the world — and the slower way of all the good ways.

For seventeen years she walked the same path between two greenhouses, one classroom, and a generation of girls who learned to love trees because she did. She named over two hundred indigenous plant specimens in the school's herbarium. Three hundred and twelve of her former students are now in the sciences.

She leaves behind her husband Mr. Yaw Adomako, two daughters Sika and Maame, and a garden in Cantonments that she tended every Saturday morning without fail.
