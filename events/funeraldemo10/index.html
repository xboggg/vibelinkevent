A Century-and-One

Her life

Naa Lamiley Quarshie was born in 1925 in a one-room hut in Teshie, the daughter of a fisherman who never came home from a Saturday voyage and a mother who raised four children alone selling salt. She married Mr. Quarshie in 1944, raised six children, buried two of them, and lived long enough to see her great-great-grandson take his first steps.

She baked bread on a coal pot until she was 96. She knew every neighbour by name on her street and the streets of three streets behind it. When the colonial government built the road past her house in 1957, she stood at the edge of it and watched the Queen's car drive by. She voted in every election since independence. She told her great-grandchildren the names of all six Presidents she had outlived.

She is survived by her four remaining children, 32 grandchildren, 87 great-grandchildren, and 11 great-great-grandchildren — at last count.
